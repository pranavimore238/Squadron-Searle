# Problem statement and research question

## Context

People often evaluate conversational systems by their visible outputs: whether an answer is fluent, relevant, or correct. Searle's Chinese Room argument challenges the inference from appropriate symbol manipulation to genuine understanding. In the thought experiment, a person who does not understand Chinese follows formal rules to produce responses to Chinese inputs. To an outside observer, the outputs may appear competent, while the person is still described as manipulating symbols by rule.

## Problem statement

The Chinese Room argument can be difficult for novice learners to grasp when introduced only through abstract philosophical prose. A small interactive demonstration may help learners distinguish a system's rule-governed input/output behavior from the stronger claim that it understands meaning. The educational challenge is to make the argument tangible without misrepresenting the toy demonstration as a model of all AI systems or presenting Searle's controversial conclusion as settled fact.

## Main project question

How can an interactive, transparent rule-based website help learners explore Searle's distinction between syntax and semantics while also understanding the limits and objections to the Chinese Room argument?

## Supporting questions

1. Can a simple rule-matching simulator make input/output symbol manipulation visible?
2. Can concise explanations distinguish syntax, semantics, and intentionality?
3. Can a short quiz assess comprehension of the central argument?
4. How can the interface communicate that the demonstration is illustrative and does not settle the debate about contemporary AI?

## Proposed answer

Develop a static educational website with a rule-based simulator, concept explanations, a discussion of at least one counterargument, and a short quiz. Evaluate it through functional checks, accessibility-oriented review, and informal task-based usability checks if participants are available and permitted. Do not claim empirical learning gains without a properly designed study and actual data.

## Limitations

The prototype uses a small deterministic rulebook, not a contemporary language model or human participant. It cannot establish whether a system has understanding, consciousness, or intentionality. The project is an educational artifact, not a controlled user study.
