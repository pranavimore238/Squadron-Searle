# Literature review and base paper

## Base paper

**Searle, John R. (1980). “Minds, brains, and programs.” Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 3(3), 417–457.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00005756

Publisher page: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/minds-brains-and-programs/DC644B47A4299C637C89772FACC2706A

Publisher-hosted PDF: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/DC644B47A4299C637C89772FACC2706A/S0140525X00005756a.pdf/div-class-title-minds

The article's central claim is that instantiating a computer program is not by itself a sufficient condition for intentionality. The Chinese Room is used to argue that a system can manipulate formal symbols according to rules without thereby demonstrating understanding of what those symbols mean. This makes the paper the most directly relevant primary source for the project.

**Use note:** The full article and its commentaries span pages 417–457 in the journal record. Cite the original article; do not copy its full text into the repository. Access to the publisher PDF may depend on access terms. Use the DOI and publisher page as the stable scholarly reference.

## Conceptual synthesis

Searle's argument targets a specific inference: that implementing the appropriate program is sufficient for understanding. The proposed simulator makes rule-governed symbol handling visible, but its toy rules do not replicate the Chinese Room in full and cannot determine the mental status of modern AI systems. The website should therefore present the thought experiment as a philosophical argument with objections, not as a software experiment that settles the issue.

## Counterargument to include

The **systems reply** argues, in broad terms, that understanding might belong to the whole system (including the rulebook, memory, and processes), even if the person implementing individual steps does not understand Chinese. Present this as a response to Searle's argument, not as a conclusive answer. The scholarly overview should be checked for a careful explanation before publication.
